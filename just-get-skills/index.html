How Skill-Links Work

The goal is not to force clever analogies. It is to find the smallest specific reusable subskill or process — or say there is no useful shortcut.

1. Resolve what you mean

Ordinary language is matched to skills, roles and tools while preserving ambiguity where it matters.

2. Decompose both skills

JGS compares abilities, mechanisms, subskills and observable behaviours rather than surface similarity.

3. Penalise generic overlap

“Focus” or “teamwork” alone is not enough. Specific mechanisms carry more explanatory weight.

4. Prefer direct, short routes

Explicit mappings come first. A two-step bridge is used only when it remains specific and useful.

5. Show the limit

Every accepted Skill-Link should say what still needs destination-specific learning and where prior habits could interfere.

6. Allow a real “no”

If the evidence or mechanism is too weak, JGS should reject the connection rather than flatter the user.

Evidence boundary: a mechanism-supported connection is not the same as a study proving that one exact skill causes improvement in another. Ranking bands order useful routes; they are not probabilities of success.
